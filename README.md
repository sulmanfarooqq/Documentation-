# Flow Vello Agency OS — 3-Person Engineering Team

## Reality first

Flow Vello is three professional software engineers, not a solo freelancer and not yet a mature agency.

The biggest commercial problem is not technical capability. It is finding businesses that have evidence of a problem worth paying to solve.

Three engineers can produce excellent software and still make zero money if the team spends its time building, planning, or contacting companies that do not currently need anything.

## The commercial operating principle

> Do not hunt for companies that might need us. Hunt for companies already showing evidence that they need something we can build.

The company therefore runs on three accountable seats:

| Seat | Primary ownership | Secondary ownership |
|---|---|---|
| A — Revenue & Solutions Lead | signal-based prospecting, sales, discovery, proposals, client relationship | architecture, high-risk technical decisions |
| B — Delivery & Automation Lead | implementation, integrations, automation, deployment | technical discovery, estimation |
| C — Product, QA & Systems Lead | product/full-stack work, QA, documentation, internal systems, proof | demos, delivery support, technical evidence |

All three are engineers. Only one person owns the pipeline every day.

## The company loop

SIGNAL → VERIFY → IDENTIFY BUYER → OUTREACH → DISCOVERY → QUALIFY → SCOPE → DEPOSIT → BUILD → QA → DEMO → HANDOFF → PROOF → REPEAT/REFERRAL

The key change is the first step.

We do not start with a random list of companies.

We start with a trigger:

- explicit project/request
- relevant hiring
- visible workflow problem
- funding/expansion/product launch
- technology/process change
- public request for help

## Stage 1 — First repeatable revenue

Do not behave like a 30-person company.

For a seven-day commercial experiment:

- one buyer
- one workflow
- one offer
- one primary hunting lane

The 50-niche package library is an offer library, not 50 simultaneous campaigns.

Initial commercial model:

- small fixed-scope project: roughly $300–$1,000 when scope supports it
- larger project only after clear discovery and payment commitment
- retainers only after a working system exists and recurring work is real

These are operating ranges, not promises.

## The signal-first client hunting system

See 16_CLIENT_HUNTING_SYSTEM/.

Its job is to answer:

1. Where is the buyer already showing demand?
2. What evidence proves the problem exists?
3. Who owns the problem?
4. What single Flow Vello offer matches it?
5. Why should we contact them now?
6. What is the smallest useful next step?

### Priority hunting lanes

1. Active project/work requests
2. Hiring signals
3. Visible operational problems
4. Growth/event triggers
5. Warm introductions/referrals

The strongest accounts have multiple independent signals.

## Daily operating rhythm

### 08:30–08:45 — Team stand-up

Each person answers:

1. What shipped yesterday?
2. What ships today?
3. What is blocked?
4. What client/revenue risk exists?

No storytelling.

### 08:45–11:00 — Revenue hunt + focused build

- A: trigger hunting, qualification, outreach, follow-ups
- B: delivery; technical validation for qualified opportunities
- C: delivery/QA/proof; evidence validation for qualified opportunities

### 11:00–11:20 — Sales/technical sync

A brings qualified opportunities.

B/C answer feasibility questions.

No speculative building.

### 11:20–13:00 — Delivery + sales

B/C build. A handles calls, proposals, discovery, client communication, or architecture.

### 14:00–16:30 — Delivery block

All three work against committed milestones.

### 16:30–17:00 — QA/review

C runs acceptance checks; B fixes; A validates against client scope.

### 17:00–17:20 — Pipeline/client update

A updates pipeline.

B updates delivery.

C updates QA/proof.

### 17:20–17:30 — Tomorrow's first actions

Every person writes one physical next action.

## Non-negotiables

- No major build without a defined client problem.
- No substantial unpaid implementation.
- No proposal without qualification.
- No outreach without a reason for contacting that account.
- No proposal based only on an imagined pain.
- No client promise without an owner.
- No delivery without acceptance criteria.
- No production release without QA.
- No new niche because the current niche was quiet for two days.
- No new tool because execution feels uncomfortable.
- No internal meeting that could be a two-line message.
- No mass outreach to compensate for a bad list.
- No fake case studies, fake metrics, fake logos, or guaranteed ROI.
- No "we're busy" when the pipeline is empty.
- No "we need more developers" when sales is the bottleneck.

## Brutal test

If the team spends a full week coding but creates no qualified conversations, proposals, or cash, the team did not have a productive agency week.

It had a productive engineering week.

Those are not the same thing.

If the team sends 500 generic messages to companies with no evidence of need, it also did not have a strong sales week.

It had a high-volume activity week.

## Scaling rule

Do not hire because three engineers feel overloaded.

Hire only when:

1. work is sold,
2. the task is repeatable,
3. the task has a written Definition of Done,
4. margin can absorb the cost,
5. the hire creates measurable capacity.

Until then, fix sales, scope, process, or prioritization.
