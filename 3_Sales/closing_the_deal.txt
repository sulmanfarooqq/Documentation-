# CLOSING — THREE-PERSON TEAM

The team does not need three salespeople.

It needs:
- A to own the commercial relationship,
- B to answer implementation feasibility,
- C to validate product/QA implications.

## Discovery

A leads:
1. What is happening now?
2. Where does the process break?
3. Who performs it?
4. How often?
5. What tools are involved?
6. What happens when it fails?
7. What does the buyer want changed?
8. Who approves?
9. What deadline matters?
10. What budget/process exists?

B joins only for technical depth.
C joins when product, UX, QA, data, or release risk matters.

## Proposal

Proposal must state:
- problem
- current workflow
- future workflow
- deliverables
- exclusions
- client responsibilities
- timeline
- acceptance criteria
- price
- payment terms
- support boundary

## Payment

For small fixed-scope projects, a common operating structure is 50% upfront and 50% before final handoff, adjusted for actual deal context.

Do not begin substantial work because "the client said yes."

A verbal yes is not cash.

## Three-person anti-scope-creep rule

If the buyer requests something outside scope:
1. A records it.
2. B estimates implementation.
3. C checks impact on QA/release.
4. A sends the change decision and price.
5. No silent free expansion.
