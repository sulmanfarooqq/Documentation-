# CLOSING A SMALL PROJECT

Your first objective is not a $10,000 contract. It is a paid project that can be delivered quickly and turned into proof.

## Discovery

Ask:
1. What happens today?
2. Where does the process break?
3. How often does it happen?
4. Who performs the manual work?
5. What tools are involved?
6. What happens when the process fails?
7. What would a useful improvement look like?
8. Who approves the work?
9. What deadline matters?
10. What budget range has been allocated?

## Scope

Write:
- problem
- current workflow
- proposed workflow
- exact deliverables
- exclusions
- client responsibilities
- timeline
- price
- payment terms
- acceptance/QA criteria

## Payment

For small projects, require an upfront deposit before production. A common structure is 50% upfront and 50% before final handoff.

Use a payment method actually available to both parties.

Never start substantial work based only on a promise to pay.

## Close

Do not pressure people. Make the decision easy:
"Based on what you showed me, this is the smallest version I would build first. It is $X, takes Y days, and includes A/B/C. If that works, I will send the scope and payment details."

A clean scope closes better than hype.
